What this shows

Quoted directly from results/findings.md:

F14 — For every (combo, horizon) cell tested — 3 combos × 4 horizons = 12 cells — the test-split bootstrap 95% CI on PR-AUC for XGBoost (reporting-only) overlaps the CI for logistic regression (reporting-only). No combo/horizon shows one model's CI fully separated from the other's, so neither model can be called better than the other with the confidence this bootstrap provides.

F15 and F16 found the same for SARIMA and SGB against both of them, at every combo and horizon (12/12 cells each) — across all four models, none is shown statistically better than another.

F15 — SARIMA's test PR-AUC CI overlaps both XGBoost's and logistic's CI for every combo at every horizon — the same "not statistically distinguishable" pattern F14 found between XGBoost and logistic themselves. SARIMA is not shown to be worse or better than either learned model by this bootstrap.

SARIMA forecasts only the reporting series itself, with no engineered lag/rolling features and no climate data. That it lands in the same statistical tier as the feature-rich ML models suggests much of their apparent skill may come from the same seasonal/autocorrelation signal a classical time-series model captures directly — consistent with F11's interpretation that Maharashtra/Dengue's performance tracks a seasonal regime rather than short-term outbreak dynamics.

F10 / F15 / F16 — Maharashtra/Dengue's lower CI bound clears its base rate for all four models at every horizon tested. Maharashtra/ADD never clears for any model, at any horizon. Karnataka/ADD clears only once across all four models and four horizons: XGBoost at h=1, and only marginally.

One combination works regardless of which model you pick; one combination fails regardless of which model you pick; the third is a single marginal exception, not a reliable result.
